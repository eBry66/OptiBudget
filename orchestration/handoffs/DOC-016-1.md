## Assumptions made

- **New database object not pre-named by DOC-015.** The document introduces
  `household_member_of(uuid)`, a `SECURITY DEFINER` helper function, used
  by every per-table policy. ARCHITECTURE.md only pre-approved
  `create_household()`. My reasoning: writing 23 independent membership
  subqueries by hand is itself a correctness risk (a single divergent
  `USING` clause), and `household_members`' own policy needs a
  non-self-referencing check to avoid Postgres's recursive-policy error
  regardless. This felt within DOC-016's remit — ARCHITECTURE.md explicitly
  defers "the actual policy for each row" to this document — but it is a
  new mechanism, not just a restatement of one ARCHITECTURE.md already
  named. Flagging for explicit confirmation rather than assuming it's
  accepted by silence.
- **No `UPDATE`/`DELETE` policy on `households`, no `DELETE` on
  `household_members`, no write policy at all on `categories`.** Grounded
  in an absence: no REQ id in `product/REQUIREMENTS.md` describes renaming
  or deleting a household, removing a member, or a household writing
  `categories`. Treated the absence as "no policy, not an invented one"
  rather than guessing a shape. If a future requirement adds any of these,
  this document needs revision to add the corresponding policy.
- **`fund_contributions`' policy uses its direct `household_id` column**,
  not a join through `co_ownership_id`, on the reasoning that
  ARCHITECTURE.md's composite foreign key already guarantees the two agree
  and the direct column is cheaper. Same one-hop simplification applied to
  `security_transactions` and `investment_income` via `positions
  .household_id` instead of the two-hop path ARCHITECTURE.md's prose
  describes through `accounts`. Both are reads of what ARCHITECTURE.md
  already constrains, not new constraints, but worth a second look since
  they diverge from the literal join chain ARCHITECTURE.md's Access
  control table states in words.
